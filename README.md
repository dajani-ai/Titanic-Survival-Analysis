# Titanic Passenger Survival Analysis
An Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) project exploring the historical Titanic dataset. This project focuses on data cleaning, transparent step-by-step calculations, and visual storytelling to uncover what factors most influenced passenger survival.

---

## Project Overview

The sinking of the Titanic is one of the most infamous maritime disasters in history. Using data analysis techniques, this project tests historical narratives such as the *"women and children first"* protocol and the impact of socioeconomic status against actual passenger data.

### Key Questions Answered:
1. **Socioeconomic Status:** Did passengers in higher ticket classes (`pclass`) have a better chance of survival?
2. **Gender Protocol:** How strongly did the *"women and children first"* rule affect survival rates between men and women?
3. **Age Distribution:** Were infants and young children prioritized during lifeboat evacuations?

---

## Key Findings & Insights

* **Gender Was the Strongest Lifeline:** Roughly **74%** of female passengers survived compared to only **21%** of male passengers, confirming that women were heavily prioritized during evacuation.
* **Ticket Class Mattered:** First-class passengers enjoyed a survival rate of **over 60%**, second-class passengers sat around **50%**, and third-class passengers faced the lowest survival odds at roughly **25%**.
* **Young Children Were Saved First:** Passengers under 6 years old had higher survival numbers than fatalities, while young adults aged 20–30 suffered the highest overall number of casualties.
